# Accessibility

How to make software and games usable by people with disabilities, whatever the platform: the laws and standards that set the baseline, building accessibility into the design system and the delivery process, testing with assistive technologies, and the design principles that every user interface shares. This is architecture guidance, not legal advice. The rules summarise the European Accessibility Act, EN 301 549 and US federal rules as their official sources describe them; other laws may add requirements, so check what applies to you with a lawyer. Each platform's how-to is in its own file. For web front ends, WCAG 2.2 AA, native HTML before ARIA and automated checks are `WEB-1` to `WEB-3` in [Web front end](../specific/web/README.md), and focus and page titles after navigation are `VUE-12` in [Vue](../specific/vue/README.md), `ANGULAR-10` in [Angular](../specific/angular/README.md), and `SVELTE-15` and `SVELTE-16` in [Svelte and SvelteKit](../specific/svelte/README.md). Games follow the Game Accessibility Guidelines (`GAMEDEV-17` to `GAMEDEV-20` in [Game development](../specific/gamedev/README.md)), with controller support in `STEAM-11` ([Steam and PC games](../specific/steam/README.md)) and pointer and gamepad input in `WEBGAME-10` and `WEBGAME-11` ([Web games](../specific/web-games/README.md)). Plain language and layouts that leave room for longer or larger text are `I18N-5` and `I18N-10` in [Internationalization and localization](localization.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or strongly recommended, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or conditional.

## Adopted references

- `10` [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) (W3C Recommendation): **the baseline** for every user interface, not only the web: testable success criteria at levels A, AA and AAA. The [WCAG 2 Overview](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/) (W3C WAI) explains the versions and how they relate to EN 301 549 and to non-web software.
- `9` [EN 301 549 V4.1.1, Accessibility requirements for ICT products and services](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/301500_301599/301549/04.01.01_60/en_301549v040101p.pdf) (ETSI, CEN and CENELEC, 2026): the harmonised European standard, with functional performance criteria and testable requirements for hardware, web content, documents, software, two-way communication and support documentation. Version 4.1.1 aligns its web, document and software clauses with WCAG 2.2 and maps them to the European Accessibility Act; it gives a presumption of conformity with the Act once it's cited in the Official Journal of the EU.
- `8` [Guidance on Applying WCAG 2 to Non-Web Information and Communications Technologies (WCAG2ICT)](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2ict-22/) (W3C Group Note, 2025): how each WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 success criterion applies to non-web documents and software, such as native mobile and desktop apps. It's informative guidance, not a standard.
- `8` [Directive (EU) 2019/882, the European Accessibility Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj) (EUR-Lex): the law itself: which products and services it covers (Article 2), the accessibility requirements (Annex I), the information service providers must give (Annex V), and the documented assessment needed to claim a fundamental alteration or disproportionate burden (Article 14). The European Commission's [overview](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/policies/justice-and-fundamental-rights/disability/european-accessibility-act-eaa_en) lists the products and services covered in plain language.
- `8` [Revised 508 Standards and 255 Guidelines](https://www.access-board.gov/ict/) (US Access Board): the accessibility requirements for ICT that US federal agencies develop, procure, maintain or use, which incorporate WCAG 2.0 Level A and AA for web content, documents and software, and add requirements for software's interoperability with assistive technology (Chapter 5).
- `7` [Fact Sheet: New Rule on the Accessibility of Web Content and Mobile Apps Provided by State and Local Governments](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/) (US Department of Justice): what the ADA Title II rule requires (WCAG 2.1 Level AA), whom it covers, its exceptions, and the compliance dates as extended in April 2026.
- `7` [Making your service accessible: an introduction](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/making-your-service-accessible-an-introduction) (GOV.UK Service Manual): how a team builds accessibility in from the start: whose job it is, research with disabled users, and what to do in each phase, from discovery to live.
- `6` [Understanding accessibility requirements for public sector bodies](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/accessibility-requirements-for-public-sector-websites-and-apps) (GOV.UK): only for UK public sector websites and apps, which must meet WCAG 2.2 AA and publish an accessibility statement, on top of the Equality Act 2010.

## Key rules

### Laws and standards

- `9` `A11Y-1` **Use WCAG 2.2 Level AA as the accessibility baseline for every user interface, not only websites: apply it to native apps, desktop software and documents through WCAG2ICT, and through EN 301 549 where EU law applies.** WCAG versions are backwards compatible, so content that meets 2.2 also meets the WCAG 2.0 and 2.1 that US rules name, and EN 301 549 V4.1.1 uses WCAG 2.2. On the web, this is `WEB-1`. ([W3C WAI, WCAG 2 Overview](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/), [W3C, WCAG2ICT](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2ict-22/), [EN 301 549 V4.1.1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/301500_301599/301549/04.01.01_60/en_301549v040101p.pdf))
- `7` `A11Y-2` **If you sell to consumers in the EU, check whether the European Accessibility Act covers your products or services, and if it does, design them to its requirements from the start.** Since 28 June 2025 it covers, among others, computers and their operating systems, smartphones, e-readers, electronic communications, e-books, consumer banking and e-commerce, including their websites and mobile apps. Service providers must explain how a service meets the requirements in their terms and conditions or an equivalent document, and microenterprises that provide services are exempt. ([Directive (EU) 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj), [European Commission, European Accessibility Act](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/policies/justice-and-fundamental-rights/disability/european-accessibility-act-eaa_en))
- `7` `A11Y-3` **In the US, check which federal accessibility rules apply to what you build: Section 508 for ICT that federal agencies develop, buy or use, the ADA Title II rule for state and local governments' websites and apps, and the CVAA for software with communication features.** Section 508 requires WCAG 2.0 AA for web content, documents and software, and the ADA rule requires WCAG 2.1 AA, also for apps that a contractor provides for a government, from 26 April 2027 or 2028 depending on the government's size. The CVAA requires electronic messaging, voice calls and video conferencing to be accessible unless that isn't achievable, including in video games released since 1 January 2019. ([US Access Board, Revised 508 Standards: E207.2 WCAG Conformance](https://www.access-board.gov/ict/#E207.2), [ADA.gov, Fact Sheet on the Title II web rule](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/), [FCC, Order DA 17-1243 on video game software](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-17-1243A1.pdf))

### Process

- `7` `A11Y-4` **Build accessibility into the design system and into the definition of done: make and test the shared components accessible once, and test each new feature for accessibility, automatically and by hand, before it counts as done.** Accessible components give every product that uses them an accessible baseline, but they don't make a product accessible on their own. Problems found late are much more expensive to fix. ([GOV.UK Design System, Accessibility strategy](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/accessibility/accessibility-strategy/#accessibility-and-design-systems), [GOV.UK Service Manual, Making your service accessible](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/making-your-service-accessible-an-introduction), [GOV.UK Service Manual, Testing for accessibility](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/testing-for-accessibility))
- `7` `A11Y-5` **Involve people with disabilities in research, design reviews and testing from the start of a project, with a range of disabilities and assistive technologies.** Don't assume that one person's feedback applies to everyone with a disability, and combine user involvement with checks against the standard. ([W3C WAI, Involving Users in Web Projects for Better, Easier Accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/planning/involving-users/), [GOV.UK Service Manual, Making your service accessible](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/making-your-service-accessible-an-introduction))
- `6` `A11Y-6` **Publish an accessibility statement for each website, app or product, saying how far it meets the standard, which parts don't and why, how to get an accessible alternative, and how to report a problem, and answer the reports you get.** Review it regularly. Public sector websites and apps in the EU and the UK must publish one, and services under the European Accessibility Act must describe how they meet its requirements. ([W3C WAI, Developing an Accessibility Statement](https://www.w3.org/WAI/planning/statements/), [GOV.UK, Publish your accessibility statement](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/make-your-website-or-app-accessible-and-publish-an-accessibility-statement#publish-your-accessibility-statement), [Directive (EU) 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj))

### Testing and tools

- `7` `A11Y-7` **Test each new feature by hand with the assistive technologies and settings people use, on every platform you ship: at least a screen reader, a keyboard or switch access, and the largest text size.** Automated checks find only part of the barriers and sometimes give false or misleading results, so run them in CI as well (`WEB-3` on the web) but don't rely on them alone. ([W3C WAI, Selecting Web Accessibility Evaluation Tools](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/tools/selecting/), [GOV.UK Service Manual, Testing with assistive technologies](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/testing-for-accessibility#testing-with-assistive-technologies), [Android, Test your app's accessibility](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/testing))
- `7` `A11Y-8` **Don't rely on accessibility overlays or widgets to make a product accessible or compliant: fix the barriers in its own code and content.** Overlays don't make a website accessible or compliant with European accessibility law, and they can interfere with the assistive technology and settings that users already have. In 2025, the US Federal Trade Commission barred accessiBe from claiming, without evidence, that its automated tool can make any website WCAG-compliant. ([European Disability Forum and IAAP, Joint statement on accessibility overlays](https://www.edf-feph.org/publications/joint-statement-on-accessibility-overlays/), [FTC, Final order requiring accessiBe to pay $1 million](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-approves-final-order-requiring-accessibe-pay-1-million))

### Interfaces and input

- `9` `A11Y-9` **Expose every control's name, role, state and value to assistive technologies through the platform's accessibility API: use the standard controls of the platform or UI toolkit, which do this for you, and implement the API yourself for custom-drawn UI, such as a canvas or a game engine's UI.** The platform how-to is in `ANDROID-9`, `IOS-14`, `FLUTTER-7`, `UNITY-CODE-15`, `GODOT-40` and `UNREAL-45`, and on the web this is `WEB-2`. ([W3C, Understanding SC 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/name-role-value.html), [EN 301 549 V4.1.1, clause 11.5.2](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/301500_301599/301549/04.01.01_60/en_301549v040101p.pdf), [US Access Board, 502.3 Accessibility Services](https://www.access-board.gov/ict/#502.3))
- `8` `A11Y-10` **Make every function work through a keyboard interface, without precise timing, because switch devices, alternative keyboards and other input that emulates a keyboard depend on it.** The only exception is a function that depends on the path of the movement, such as freehand drawing. For games, controller support and remapping are `STEAM-11` and `GAMEDEV-18`. ([W3C, Understanding SC 2.1.1 Keyboard](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html), [Android, Test your app's accessibility: Switch Access](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/testing#switch-access))
- `7` `A11Y-11` **Never convey information or instructions by colour alone or by sound alone, or only through shape, size, position or orientation: add text, icons, patterns or captions.** In games, this is `GAMEDEV-19`. ([W3C, Understanding SC 1.4.1 Use of Color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html), [W3C, Understanding SC 1.3.3 Sensory Characteristics](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/sensory-characteristics.html))
- `7` `A11Y-12` **Follow the platform's accessibility settings instead of overriding them, such as text size, bold text, contrast and colour filters, reduced motion and forced colours.** EN 301 549 and Section 508 require this of software that isn't designed to be isolated from its platform; you can add your own settings, as long as one mode follows the system's. On the web, use the `prefers-reduced-motion` and `forced-colors` media features; text size on Android, iOS and Flutter is `ANDROID-10`, `IOS-14` and `FLUTTER-8`. ([EN 301 549 V4.1.1, clause 11.7 User preferences](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/301500_301599/301549/04.01.01_60/en_301549v040101p.pdf), [US Access Board, 503.2 User Preferences](https://www.access-board.gov/ict/#503.2), [MDN, prefers-reduced-motion](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion), [MDN, forced-colors](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/forced-colors))

### Time, motion and flashing

- `8` `A11Y-13` **Don't show anything that flashes more than three times in any one second, unless the flashes stay below the general flash and red flash thresholds.** Flashing can cause seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy, so check video, cutscenes and effects with a flash analysis tool. ([W3C, Understanding SC 2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html))
- `7` `A11Y-14` **Give people enough time: let them turn off, adjust or extend each time limit, and pause, stop or hide anything that moves, blinks or scrolls on its own for more than five seconds.** Warn before time runs out and allow at least 20 seconds to extend it with a simple action, except in real-time events and where the limit is essential to the activity. For people with vestibular disorders, also let users turn off animation that their actions trigger, for example by following the reduced motion setting. ([W3C, Understanding SC 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/timing-adjustable.html), [W3C, Understanding SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html), [W3C, Understanding SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html))

## Tutorials and further reading

- `7` [Digital Accessibility Foundations](https://www.w3.org/WAI/courses/foundations-course/) (W3C WAI and UNESCO IITE, on edX): a free, self-paced course of 16 to 20 hours for technical and non-technical learners: how people with disabilities use digital technology, the business case, the principles and standards, simple checks, and how to start integrating accessibility into design and development.
- `7` [How People with Disabilities Use the Web](https://www.w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/) (W3C WAI): stories of users, the range of abilities and the barriers people meet, and the tools and techniques they use. Read it before designing for or testing with assistive technologies.
- `6` [Web Accessibility Tutorials](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/) (W3C WAI): worked examples for page structure, menus, images, tables, forms and carousels. They're written for the web, but the concepts apply to other formats too.
