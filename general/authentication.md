# Authentication and sessions

How users prove who they are and how an application keeps them signed in: identity providers, OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect, sessions and tokens in browser apps, passwords, multi-factor authentication, passkeys and account recovery. What a signed-in user may do (authorization, `SEC-1`), secrets and security logging are covered in [Security](security.md). For Next.js apps, also follow [Next.js](../specific/nextjs/README.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or strongly recommended, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or conditional.

## Adopted references

- `10` [NIST SP 800-63B-4, Authentication and Authenticator Management](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html) (2025): **the baseline** for passwords, multi-factor and phishing-resistant authenticators, syncable authenticators such as passkeys, reauthentication, sessions and account recovery.
- `9` [OWASP ASVS 5.0, V6 Authentication](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS/blob/master/5.0/en/0x15-V6-Authentication.md), [V7 Session Management](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS/blob/master/5.0/en/0x16-V7-Session-Management.md) and [V10 OAuth and OIDC](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS/blob/master/5.0/en/0x19-V10-OAuth-and-OIDC.md): verifiable requirements by level. Use them as the checklist, as [Security](security.md) does with ASVS as a whole.
- `9` [RFC 9700, Best Current Practice for OAuth 2.0 Security](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9700) (IETF, 2025): the current security rules for OAuth clients, authorization servers and resource servers.
- `8` [RFC 10017, OAuth 2.0 for Browser-Based Applications](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc10017) (IETF, 2026): the architecture patterns for single-page apps (Backend for Frontend, token-mediating backend, browser-only client), their threats, and the patterns to avoid.
- `8` [OWASP Authentication Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html) and the cheat sheets for [Session Management](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html), [Password Storage](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html), [Multifactor Authentication](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Multifactor_Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html), [Passkey Security](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Passkey_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html) and [Forgot Password](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Forgot_Password_Cheat_Sheet.html): practical implementation guidance for each part of sign-in.
- `8` [OpenID Connect Core 1.0](https://openid.net/specs/openid-connect-core-1_0.html) (OpenID Foundation): the identity layer on top of OAuth 2.0 for sign-in and single sign-on.
- `8` [Web Authentication (WebAuthn) Level 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/) (W3C Recommendation, 2026): the browser API behind passkeys. [passkeys.dev](https://passkeys.dev/docs/intro/what-are-passkeys/) (W3C community group and FIDO Alliance) explains how to adopt them.

## Key rules

### Identity providers and protocols

- `9` `AUTH-1` **In browser apps, keep tokens out of reach of JavaScript: use a server-side session in a cookie, or a Backend for Frontend (BFF) that holds the OAuth tokens.** Don't keep tokens or session IDs in `localStorage` or `sessionStorage`, where any XSS can read them; when the frontend and its backend share a domain, a cookie session is enough and OAuth usually isn't needed between them. ([RFC 10017, Backend for Frontend](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc10017#section-6.1), [Single-domain applications](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc10017#section-7.1), [OWASP, The localStorage API](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html#the-localstorage-api))
- `9` `AUTH-2` **Use the authorization code flow with PKCE for every OAuth client. Don't use the implicit grant, and never use the resource owner password credentials grant.** Public clients must use PKCE, and it's recommended for confidential clients too. ([RFC 9700, Recommendations](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9700#section-2), [OWASP OAuth 2.0 Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/OAuth2_Cheat_Sheet.html))
- `8` `AUTH-3` **Use an established identity provider, or your framework's built-in authentication and session management, instead of writing your own.** Keep the library up to date: your code still has to configure it and check what it returns. ([OWASP, Built-in session management implementations](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html#built-in-session-management-implementations), [OWASP, Passkeys: use a maintained library](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Passkey_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#use-a-maintained-library), [OWASP, MFA: consider using a third-party service](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Multifactor_Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html#consider-using-a-third-party-service))
- `8` `AUTH-4` **Sign users in through an external identity provider with OpenID Connect, not plain OAuth, and identify each federated user by issuer and subject (`iss` and `sub`).** Don't link an external identity to an existing account just because the email address matches. ([OpenID Connect Core, Claim stability and uniqueness](https://openid.net/specs/openid-connect-core-1_0.html#ClaimStability), [OWASP, Secure federated account linking](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html#secure-federated-account-linking))

### Sessions

- `9` `AUTH-5` **Set session cookies with `Secure`, `HttpOnly` and `SameSite=Strict` or `Lax`, and prefer the `__Host-` name prefix.** ([OWASP, Session Management: Cookies](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html#cookies), [MDN, Set-Cookie: Cookie prefixes](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Reference/Headers/Set-Cookie#cookie_prefixes))
- `9` `AUTH-6` **Issue a new session ID when a user signs in, re-authenticates or changes privilege level, and invalidate the old one.** This prevents session fixation. ([OWASP, Renew the session ID after any privilege level change](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html#renew-the-session-id-after-any-privilege-level-change), [ASVS 5.0, V7 Session Management](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS/blob/master/5.0/en/0x16-V7-Session-Management.md))
- `8` `AUTH-7` **Enforce idle and absolute session timeouts on the server, and invalidate the session on the server when the user logs out.** Clearing the cookie isn't enough, and timeouts tracked by the client can be manipulated. ([OWASP, Session expiration](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html#session-expiration), [OWASP, Logout button](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html#logout-button))
- `7` `AUTH-8` **Make sessions revocable: end all of a user's sessions when the account is disabled, and offer to end the other sessions after a password or MFA change.** Self-contained tokens such as JWTs stay valid until they expire unless you add a revocation mechanism, such as a list of terminated tokens. ([ASVS 5.0, V7 Session Management](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS/blob/master/5.0/en/0x16-V7-Session-Management.md))
- `7` `AUTH-9` **Ask for the current credentials again before sensitive changes, such as a new password or email address, and before sensitive transactions.** ([OWASP, Require re-authentication for sensitive features](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html#require-re-authentication-for-sensitive-features))

### Passwords and multi-factor authentication

- `9` `AUTH-10` **Store passwords only as salted hashes from a slow password-hashing function: Argon2id, scrypt, bcrypt or PBKDF2.** Fast hashes such as SHA-256 let attackers test guesses quickly. ([OWASP Password Storage Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html), [NIST SP 800-63B-4, Password verifiers](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html#passwordver))
- `9` `AUTH-11` **Follow NIST's password rules: require at least 15 characters for passwords used alone (8 with MFA), allow at least 64, and reject passwords on a blocklist of common and breached ones.** Don't impose composition rules, such as a mix of character types, or periodic password changes. ([NIST SP 800-63B-4, Passwords](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html#password), [OWASP, Implement proper password strength controls](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html#implement-proper-password-strength-controls))
- `9` `AUTH-12` **Offer multi-factor authentication to all users, and require it for administrators and other privileged accounts.** ([OWASP, MFA quick recommendations](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Multifactor_Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html#quick-recommendations))
- `7` `AUTH-13` **Offer a phishing-resistant authenticator, preferably passkeys. When a passkey counts as a second factor, require user verification and check its flag on the server.** Passwords and one-time codes can be relayed by a phishing proxy; WebAuthn binds the sign-in to the real origin. ([NIST SP 800-63B-4, AAL2 requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html#aal2req), [OWASP, MFA: phishing-resistant control](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Multifactor_Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html#general-mitigation-primary-phishing-resistant-control), [OWASP, MFA: passkeys](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Multifactor_Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html#passkeys))
- `7` `AUTH-14` **Give the same response, in about the same time, whether the account doesn't exist, the password is wrong or the account is locked, and limit failed attempts per account.** ([OWASP, Authentication responses](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html#authentication-responses), [OWASP, Login throttling](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html#login-throttling), [NIST SP 800-63B-4, Rate limiting](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html#throttle))

### Account recovery

- `8` `AUTH-15` **Protect account recovery as strongly as sign-in: use random, single-use, short-lived reset tokens, answer the same whether or not the account exists, and notify the user after every recovery.** Don't let email or SMS recovery silently bypass a stronger sign-in, such as passkeys, on high-risk accounts. ([OWASP Forgot Password Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Forgot_Password_Cheat_Sheet.html), [OWASP, Passkeys: account recovery](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Passkey_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#account-recovery-and-bootstrap), [NIST SP 800-63B-4, Account recovery](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html#recovery))
- `6` `AUTH-16` **Encourage users to register more than one passkey or authenticator, or to keep single-use recovery codes, so that losing one device doesn't force a weaker recovery.** ([OWASP, Passkeys: account recovery](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Passkey_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html#account-recovery-and-bootstrap), [OWASP, Resetting MFA](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Multifactor_Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html#resetting-mfa))

## How to enforce

Tools that check the key rules above automatically, in CI, the IDE or a commit hook. "Partly" means the tool covers only some of what the rule asks.

- `6` [ZAP baseline scan](https://www.zaproxy.org/docs/docker/baseline-scan/): runs ZAP's passive scan against a deployed app in CI. The rules [Cookie No HttpOnly Flag](https://www.zaproxy.org/docs/alerts/10010/), [Cookie Without Secure Flag](https://www.zaproxy.org/docs/alerts/10011/) and [Cookie without SameSite Attribute](https://www.zaproxy.org/docs/alerts/10054/) flag missing cookie attributes. Partly checks `AUTH-5`.
- `6` [CodeQL, Sensitive server cookie exposed to the client](https://codeql.github.com/codeql-query-help/javascript/js-client-exposed-cookie/) (JavaScript) and Failure to use secure cookies ([Python](https://codeql.github.com/codeql-query-help/python/py-insecure-cookie/), [Java](https://codeql.github.com/codeql-query-help/java/java-insecure-cookie/)): code-scanning queries that flag cookies set without `HttpOnly` or `Secure`. Partly checks `AUTH-5`.
- `5` [CodeQL, Use of password hash with insufficient computational effort](https://codeql.github.com/codeql-query-help/javascript/js-insufficient-password-hash/) (JavaScript) and [Use of a broken or weak cryptographic hashing algorithm on sensitive data](https://codeql.github.com/codeql-query-help/python/py-weak-sensitive-data-hashing/) (Python): code-scanning queries that flag passwords hashed with fast algorithms such as MD5 or SHA-1. Partly checks `AUTH-10`.
